### [The Short Leash AI Coding Method For Beating Fable](https://blog.okturtles.org/2026/07/short-leash-ai-method/)

The author describes staying in the loop as the AI writes code, steering constantly. One thing I'd add to their toolkit: ask the AI to produce diagrams. ERDs, state diagrams, sequence diagrams -- whatever the task calls for. Just by drawing them, it often finds bugs in the design, before I even review them.

### [DON'T YOU MEAN EXTINCT?](https://fabiensanglard.net/extinct/index.html)

If we are at the cusp of a paradigm change, it makes sense that we need to evolve. To adapt. Just like the stop-motion animators did. It turns out they had more to offer than stop-motion know-how. They also knew a great deal about dynamics, and about what movement looks natural.

### [Should You Use AI for a Task? Here’s a Simple Way to Decide](https://www.schneier.com/blog/archives/2026/07/should-you-use-ai-for-a-task-heres-a-simple-way-to-decide.html)

Really useful heuristic: Is it work or the gym?

An example that comes to mind: I can ask AI to summarize the instruction manual for my Instant Pot. The manual was badly written in the first place, and all I need to know is which button to press. But asking AI to compress a novel into a paragraph? What's the point? The enjoyment of a book is reading it, learning what happens at the book's own pace.
